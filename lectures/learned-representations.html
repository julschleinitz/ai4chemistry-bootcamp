Lecture 6

Learned Representations

Unsupervised learning, autoencoders and VAEs, self-supervised pre-training and transfer learning — what happens when the model, not you, chooses the features.

Jules Schleinitz
August 12, 2026 · 13:30 – 15:00
RSC 275
90 min
Back to schedule
Slides (PDF) Recording (after bootcamp)

Learning Objectives

  • Describe what unsupervised learning is, and use PCA, t-SNE/UMAP and Butina clustering on a molecular dataset — while reading the resulting maps honestly
  • Explain why a Bemis–Murcko scaffold split is not optional, and what a random split hides
  • Read an autoencoder or VAE figure in a paper and say what is and is not being claimed about the latent space
  • Contrast the two families of self-supervised pre-training — masked prediction and contrastive learning
  • Choose between frozen, partial and full fine-tuning from your dataset size, and recognise negative transfer when it happens
  • Measure, rather than assume, the training-set size at which a pre-trained representation stops beating ECFP + random forest

Key Takeaways

  • Labels are the bottleneck, not molecules. We can enumerate 1011 molecules and have measured 103. Unsupervised and self-supervised learning exist to spend the first to make up for the second.

  • Unsupervised methods find real structure — but a 2-D map is a picture of your fingerprint. Word2vec on 3.3 M abstracts recovers the periodic table; masked pre-training recovers 82% of 792 reaction classes with no labels. Equally, t-SNE produces convincing clusters from pure noise.

  • The VAE's decoder disappointed; its encoder changed the field. As generators, molecular VAEs lose to a 30-second graph genetic algorithm and, on some benchmarks, to random screening. The pretext-task-then-keep-the-encoder pattern is what survived.

  • Validity was the easy metric. JT-VAE and SELFIES reach 100% validity by construction, not by learning — and the model with perfect validity often matches real chemistry worst.

  • Pre-training is a prior. Worth ~3× at a hundred labels (NMR transfer: 0.43 vs 1.35 ppm), worth nothing by a thousand, and occasionally worth less than nothing — pre-training a GAT cost 6.5 points of ROC-AUC.

  • Always run ECFP + random forest, and always split by scaffold. In the largest independent replication (25 models × 25 datasets, 2025), almost nothing beat that baseline significantly.
