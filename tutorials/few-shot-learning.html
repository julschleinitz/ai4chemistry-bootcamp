Tutorial 7

Few-Shot Learning

Reproduce Han & Choi's transfer-learning approach to 1H NMR chemical shift prediction: pretrain a GNN on computed (DFT) data, fine-tune on a handful of experimental molecules, and see why that beats training on experimental data alone.

What you will learn

  • Build a message-passing GNN that predicts NMR shielding/shift directly from 3D atomic structure, and train it on a small subset of real DFT-computed data
  • Reproduce the paper's central result: fine-tuning accuracy on experimental 1H shifts improves as you increase the fraction of experimental data used, even at 1–10%
  • Understand exactly what fine-tuning changes (reused weights, re-initialized readout heads, frozen early layers, LR warm-up + cosine decay) and explore the effect interactively
  • Compare transfer learning against training on experimental data only, training on a narrowed "common molecules" chemical space, and a classical k-NN/HOSE-code baseline
  • Predict on a held-out class test set and submit to the live leaderboard

Instructions

Getting started

Open the Colab notebook using the button above. Run each cell in order; cells marked Exercise require you to fill in code.

  1. Build the architecture (a). Implement the edge-network + GRU message-passing GNN from Han & Choi (2021), and pretrain it on a small real subset of DFT-computed shielding constants (QM9NMR case-study molecules).

  2. More data, less error (b). Fine-tune the pretrained model on increasing fractions (1%, 4%, 7%, 10%, ...) of a real experimental 1H NMR dataset (NMRShiftDB2-derived). Plot MAE vs. fraction and compare its shape to Figure 1c of the paper.

  3. Fine-tuning hyperparameters (c). Use the interactive widget to vary learning rate, number of frozen layers, warm-up length, and epochs. Find a setting that clearly under- or over-adapts.

  4. Comparisons (d). Train an experimental-only model (no pretraining), a common-molecules-only model (H/C/N/O/F only), and a k-NN/HOSE-code baseline at the same data budget. Compare all four MAEs.

  5. Challenge. Predict delta(1H) for the fixed class test set, compute your MAE, and run the notebook's submit cell — it POSTs your score straight from Colab to appear on the live leaderboard below.

Questions

Answer these questions as you work through the notebook. Discuss with your neighbour — some have no single right answer.

    Why does the pretrained model's MAE for σ(H) come out so much smaller than for σ(C), σ(N), σ(O), σ(F)? (Hint: look at the spread of each element's shielding-constant distribution, not just the model.)

    Core
